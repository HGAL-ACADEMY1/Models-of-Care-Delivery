Models of Nursing Care Delivery

Hello there! 👋 Welcome to today’s session. I’m so excited to guide you through this topic. Think of us sitting in a comfortable classroom, just chatting about how hospitals actually work.

Today, we are going to explore the Models of Nursing Care Delivery. That sounds like a big, fancy title, right? 🧐 But honestly, it just means: "How do nurses organize themselves to take care of patients?"

Imagine you want to clean a huge house. You could do it all by yourself, OR you could get a team where one person sweeps, one washes dishes, and one dusts. Hospitals are the same! They have different "strategies" for organizing nurses to make sure everyone gets well. 🏥

Ready? Let's dive into the first model! 👇

1. Functional Nursing Method

Let’s start with the Functional Nursing method. Imagine an assembly line in a factory. 🏭 Everyone has one specific job to do over and over again.

In this model, the focus is purely on completing tasks. We use different levels of staff depending on how hard the job is. The idea is to be super efficient, like a machine! ⚙️

Here, staff members with less training might do the simpler jobs, while the professional nurses handle the complex stuff. The "Head Nurse" is like the factory manager giving out orders.

For example: 📝
• One nurse might go around giving everyone their medicine.
• Another nurse checks everyone’s blood pressure.
• Another nurse might handle discharge planning (helping patients get ready to go home).
• A nursing assistant might just be responsible for making beds or giving baths to everyone.

Why do we do this? (The Good Stuff) ✅
It’s very efficient! A small number of nurses can take care of a HUGE number of patients because everyone sticks to their specific task. It also saves money because you can use less expensive assistants for simple tasks.

But... there’s a catch. (The Bad Stuff) ⚠️
Because everyone is just doing "tasks," the care can feel fragmented. It can feel a bit robotic or depersonalized. Imagine 5 different people coming into your room in one hour! Also, because no one person is responsible for the whole patient, emotional needs might get ignored. 😢

Okay, let’s now move on to a model that tries to fix that "robotic" feeling...

2. Team Nursing

Think of your favorite sports team, like a football team. ⚽ You have a Captain, and then you have other players.

Team Nursing is exactly like that! It focuses on giving individualized care through a group or "team" of different people—professionals, technical staff, and assistants.

The Registered Nurse (RN) is the Captain (Team Leader). 👩‍✈️ They are responsible for supervising the team and planning the care. They handle the really sick patients who need skilled help.

Then, licensed practical nurses might help patients who aren't as sick, while nursing assistants help with meals and making beds. But they all work together for a specific group of patients.

Why do we like this? 👍
It is cost-effective and helps the Registered Nurse get more done. It also feels a bit more personal than the "Functional" factory method we just talked about.

However... 🛑
The major disadvantage is that some people believe that if patients are very sick (high acuity), there isn't much work that can be safely "delegated" or handed off to the assistants. Also, if the Team Leader isn't a good communicator, the team falls apart!

Now that we understand the "Team," let’s talk about a model where ONE nurse takes charge...

3. Primary Nursing

This is the "Super Nurse" model! 🦸‍♀️ In Primary Nursing, one professional nurse is responsible for the TOTAL care of a patient 24 hours a day, 7 days a week.

Wait, does that mean the nurse never sleeps? 😴 No! But even when that nurse goes home, they are still the "boss" of that patient's care plan. Other nurses (associates) follow their instructions when they are away.

The Primary Nurse assesses needs, sets goals, and makes the plan. They handle everything: teaching, decision-making, and advocating for the patient.

The Best Part: 🌟
Continuity! The patient knows exactly who is in charge of them. The care is very consistent and comprehensive.

The Hard Part: 💰
You need a LOT of highly skilled Registered Nurses for this to work. That makes this system very expensive to maintain.

Let’s now dive into a model that puts the patient right in the center...

4. Patient-Centered Nursing

The name says it all! The central figure here is the Patient. 🛌

In the old days, if you needed an X-ray, you went to the X-ray room. If you needed blood drawn, you went to the lab. In Patient-Centered Nursing, we try to bring all those services to the patient!

The idea is to move services closer to the bedside. This reduces the number of steps and people involved. It promotes "cross-training," meaning workers learn multiple skills (like a nurse who can also do an ECG test). 📉

This reduces stress for the patient because they don't have to be wheeled around the hospital all day!

Next, we’ll look at the "Grandfather" of nursing models...

5. Case Method

This is one of the earliest models, also called Total Care. 🕰️

In this method, one nurse is assigned to a group of patients and handles EVERYTHING for them during an 8 to 12-hour shift. They assess, plan, and give all the care.

"Wait," you might ask, "Isn't that just Primary Nursing?" 🤔

Good question! The difference is the time frame. In Primary Nursing, the nurse is responsible 24/7. In the Case Method, the nurse is only responsible for the patient during that specific shift. Once they clock out, their responsibility ends.

At this point, it’s important to consider how education changes a nurse's role...

6. Differentiated Practice Model

This model is all about Qualifications. 🎓

It recognizes that nurses have different levels of education. A nurse with a Diploma has different training than a nurse with a Bachelor’s degree, or a Master’s degree, or a PhD.

So, in this model, we assign roles based on education and skill. It creates specific job descriptions for everyone. It clearly separates what a licensed nurse does versus an unlicensed assistant. This helps give quality care at an affordable cost.

Finally, let’s wrap up with two variations...

7. Total Client Care and Modular Nursing

These are cousins of the Primary Nursing system. 👯

Total Client Care: Just like we discussed, one nurse does everything for the shift. But, the responsibility changes from shift to shift. We use RNs for the hard cases and LPNs (Licensed Practical Nurses) for the simpler ones.

Modular Nursing: Imagine dividing the hospital ward into small "pods" or "modules." 🧩 A group of nurses is assigned to a specific module (a small geographic area of the ward). This ensures that the patients in that corner get cared for by the same faces regularly.

And there you have it! Those are the main ways we organize nursing care to help people get better! 🎉
